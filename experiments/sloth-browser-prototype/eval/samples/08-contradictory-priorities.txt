Statistics for Psychology: course notes and exam hints (compiled from three different years)

Note from 2023 handout. Hypothesis testing is the most important topic in the course, because every later method depends on it. The exam always contains a long question on hypothesis testing. Students should be able to state a null hypothesis, choose a significance level and interpret a p-value.

Note from 2024 handout. Hypothesis testing will be assessed only lightly this year, since the new syllabus moves the emphasis to effect sizes and confidence intervals. Confidence intervals give a range of plausible values for a population parameter, and an effect size such as Cohen's d measures how large a difference is in standard deviation units.

Note from 2025 handout. Descriptive statistics are not examined, so skip them: they are assumed knowledge. The mean, median and standard deviation summarise a sample, and a histogram shows its distribution.

Regression. Simple linear regression fits a straight line to predict one variable from another, and the coefficient of determination shows how much variance the line explains. Correlation is not causation; a strong correlation can arise from a third variable.

Correlation and association. The Pearson correlation coefficient measures the strength and direction of a linear relationship between two variables, from minus one to plus one.

Sampling. A random sample lets us generalise to a population, while a convenience sample may be biased. Larger samples reduce the standard error of the mean.

ANOVA. Analysis of variance compares the means of three or more groups using the F statistic. It is mentioned in the syllabus but the lecturer said it is probably not worth revising for this exam.
