BIO 101: Introduction to Cell and Molecular Biology. Study guide for the midterm.

The midterm covers the twenty-two items below. Items marked (core) will be examined directly; everything else may appear in short-answer questions.

- Cell theory: all living things are made of cells, and cells come only from existing cells. (core)
- Prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells: prokaryotes lack a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles, while eukaryotes have both.
- Plasma membrane structure: a phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins, described by the fluid mosaic model. (core)
- Membrane transport: diffusion, osmosis, facilitated diffusion and active transport across membranes.
- The nucleus: stores DNA, is surrounded by a double envelope with pores, and contains the nucleolus.
- Ribosomes: the sites of protein synthesis, free in the cytosol or bound to the rough endoplasmic reticulum.
- Endomembrane system: the endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus and vesicles modify and ship proteins.
- Mitochondria: produce most of the cell's ATP through oxidative phosphorylation. (core)
- Chloroplasts: carry out photosynthesis in plant cells using chlorophyll.
- The cytoskeleton: microtubules, microfilaments and intermediate filaments give shape and enable movement.
- Enzymes: biological catalysts that lower activation energy and are affected by temperature and pH. (core)
- Cellular respiration: glycolysis, the citric acid cycle and the electron transport chain release energy from glucose. (core)
- Photosynthesis: the light reactions and the Calvin cycle convert light energy into sugar.
- DNA structure: a double helix of nucleotides held together by complementary base pairing.
- DNA replication: a semiconservative process using helicase, primase and DNA polymerase.
- Transcription: RNA polymerase copies a gene into messenger RNA in the nucleus.
- Translation: ribosomes read messenger RNA codons and assemble amino acids into a protein. (core)
- Gene regulation: operons in bacteria and transcription factors in eukaryotes control which genes are expressed.
- The cell cycle: interphase, mitosis and cytokinesis, controlled by checkpoints.
- Meiosis: two divisions that halve the chromosome number and create genetic variation.
- Mendelian genetics: dominant and recessive alleles, Punnett squares and the laws of segregation and independent assortment.
- Mutations: changes in the DNA sequence, including substitutions, insertions and deletions.

Exam format: 40 multiple choice questions and 4 short answers. The core items together carry 70 percent of the marks.
